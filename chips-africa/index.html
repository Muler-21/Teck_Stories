MODULE 1Start with a problem · ከችግር ይጀምሩ

Start with a real problem

True story

In 2001–2002, a severe drought and famine hit Malawi. William Kamkwamba, 14, had to leave school because his family could not pay the fees. He kept learning in a small village library, where he found a textbook called Using Energy with a picture of a wind turbine. From a bicycle dynamo, a tractor fan, blue-gum poles and other scrap, he built a windmill that lit four bulbs in his home, with homemade switches and a circuit breaker made from nails. A later windmill pumped water to irrigate crops.

William did not start with money, a lab or a degree. He started with a problem his family felt every day (darkness and hunger), a library book and the scrap around him. That is the first rule of building: a real problem plus what you have.

The science

A bicycle dynamo is a small generator. When a magnet spins near a coil of copper wire, the changing magnetic field pushes electrons along the wire. This is electromagnetic induction, discovered by Michael Faraday in 1831. Wind turns the blades, the blades turn the magnet, and motion becomes electricity.

MODULE 2The science of a chip · የቺፕ ሳይንስ

How a semiconductor works

True story

Kelvin Doe grew up in Freetown, Sierra Leone, where the power came on only rarely. From about age 10 he searched scrap heaps for parts. When he realised all his money went on batteries, he built his own from acid, baking soda and metal. He built a sound mixer from scrap transistors, then a generator and an FM transmitter, and ran a community radio station as “DJ Focus”. In 2012, at 15, he became the youngest person invited to MIT's Visiting Practitioner programme.

Kelvin learned by taking things apart and testing them. To go further, from using parts to understanding them, you need the science inside every chip:

The science
  • Silicon has 4 outer electrons. In a pure crystal, each one is locked in a bond with a neighbour, so pure silicon conducts electricity poorly.
  • Doping: replace one atom in about a million with phosphorus (5 outer electrons) and a spare electron is free to move: n-type. Use boron (3) instead and you leave a gap, a hole, that also moves: p-type.
  • p-n junction: join p and n and current flows easily in one direction only. That is a diode. An LED is a diode that gives out light when electrons fall into holes.
  • Transistor: a small voltage on its control pin opens or closes a path for a bigger current. It is a switch with no moving parts. A modern chip holds billions of them.

Build 1: a fruit battery that proves the diode

You need: 3 lemons (or potatoes), 3 galvanised (zinc-coated) nails, 3 pieces of copper wire or copper coins, 4 short wires, and 1 red LED from an old toy or charger.

  1. Push one nail and one copper piece into each lemon, about 2 cm apart, not touching.
  2. Connect them in a chain: copper of lemon 1 to zinc of lemon 2, copper of lemon 2 to zinc of lemon 3.
  3. Touch the LED's long leg to the free copper end and its short leg to the free zinc end. In a dark room it glows faintly.
  4. Now turn the LED around. It stays dark. You have just seen a p-n junction allow current one way only.
The science

Zinc gives up electrons more easily than copper. The lemon juice (an acid) lets ions move between them, so electrons flow through the wire from zinc to copper. Each lemon gives about 0.9 volts. A red LED needs about 1.8–2 volts, so three lemons in series are just enough.

3 × 0.9 V ≈ 2.7 V > 1.8 V (red LED)

The materials, and the treasure in waste

True story

Near Lubumbashi in DR Congo stands Big Hill, about 10 million tonnes of slag left by a century of mining. In 2024, Gécamines' subsidiary STL began recovering germanium from it at a new local plant, with technical support from the Belgian company Umicore, and exported the first concentrates processed inside the country. What others had thrown away became a chip material.

The heart of a chip is silicon from quartz sand. Around it are small amounts of other elements for wiring, switching, joining and cleaning. Many are mined in Africa:

Your own mine: old electronics

Every broken radio, phone charger, toy or computer is a box of free parts: transistors, resistors, LEDs, capacitors, motors, magnets, switches, wire and small speakers. Read the codes printed on transistors (for example BC547, 2N2222 or C1815) and look them up online to learn what they do.

Safety first
  • Never burn cables or circuit boards to get copper. The smoke is toxic.
  • Never open microwave ovens, old tube TVs or power supplies that were plugged in recently. They hold charges that can kill.
  • Do not puncture, bend or heat lithium batteries. Swollen batteries can catch fire.
  • Old solder contains lead: work with air flowing and wash your hands after.
  • Only work with batteries (up to 12 volts), never with the 220-volt mains.

MODULE 4Build a real sensor · እውነተኛ ዳሳሽ ይገንቡ

Build a dry-soil alarm from scrap

True story

Brian Gitta, a computer science student at Makerere University in Uganda, kept missing lectures because of malaria, and once it took four blood tests to diagnose him. With his team he built Matibabu, a device that shines a red light through a finger and sends a result to a phone, with no blood needed. In 2018, at 24, he won the Royal Academy of Engineering's Africa Prize for Engineering Innovation (£25,000). His team was honest that the fifth prototype was about 80% accurate, and kept improving it.

Brian turned a personal pain into a sensor. You can start the same way with farming. The circuit below tells a farmer when the soil is dry. It uses one transistor and no microcontroller, and every part can come from scrap.

The science

Water in soil carries dissolved salts (ions), so wet soil conducts electricity and dry soil almost does not. R1 and the soil form a voltage divider at the transistor's base. A silicon transistor switches on when its base is above about 0.6 V.

Vbase = 4.5 V × Rsoil ÷ (R1 + Rsoil)

Wet soil, say 5 kΩ: 4.5 × 5 ÷ 52 ≈ 0.43 V → transistor off → LED off.
Dry soil, say 500 kΩ: the divider would give 4.5 × 500 ÷ 547 ≈ 4.1 V, far above 0.6 V, so current flows into the base, the transistor turns on (its base then settles near 0.7 V) and the LED lights.

The LED resistor follows Ohm's law (V = I × R): (4.5 V − 2 V) ÷ 220 Ω ≈ 11 mA, a safe current for a red LED.

Build 2: step by step

  1. Identify the transistor's pins (C, B, E) from its code and datasheet.
  2. Connect E to the battery's minus (−).
  3. Connect R1 (47 kΩ) from plus (+) to B.
  4. Connect one nail wire to B and the other nail wire to minus (−).
  5. Connect R2 (220 Ω) from plus (+) to the LED's long leg, and the LED's short leg to C.
  6. Push both nails 5 cm deep into soil, about 5 cm apart. Test in a cup of dry soil, then add water.

Tuning: if the LED stays on even in wet soil, use a larger R1 (100 kΩ). If it never lights in dry soil, use a smaller R1 (22 kΩ). Soils differ, so testing is part of the science.

The science

Why the nails rust: direct current through wet soil causes electrolysis, which corrodes the probes. Add the push button so power flows only when you check. Galvanised or stainless nails last longer.

Founder's tool

Build the smallest version, measure, improve. This alarm is a minimum viable product (MVP): the simplest thing that solves the problem well enough to learn from. Like Brian's team, measure how well it works and be honest about the numbers.

Your mission

Build the alarm. Measure soil resistance with a multimeter (borrow one) in dry, damp and wet soil and record it. Then test the alarm for one week in a real garden or field and write down every problem you see.

MODULE 5How chips are made · ቺፕ እንዴት ይመረታል

From sand to chip in eight steps

Your alarm uses one transistor. A factory puts billions on a chip smaller than a fingernail. A leading-edge chip passes through 500 to 1,000 process steps and 80 to 120 layers, over three to four months, in cleanrooms where one speck of dust can ruin it. The pattern-printing step needs an extreme-ultraviolet (EUV) machine that only ASML in the Netherlands builds: about $200 million each, and about $380 million for the newest High-NA version.

The science

The chemistry of pure silicon:

SiO₂ + 2C → Si + 2CO (electric furnace, ~2,000 °C, ~98–99% pure)

Si + 3HCl → SiHCl₃ + H₂ (make a liquid that can be distilled)

SiHCl₃ + H₂ → Si + 3HCl (on hot rods, ~1,100 °C: 99.9999999%+)

Then a single crystal is pulled slowly from molten silicon (the Czochralski method) and sliced into wafers.

የእርስዎ ማንቂያ አንድ ትራንዚስተር ይጠቀማል። ፋብሪካ ደግሞ ከጣት ጥፍር ባነሰ ቺፕ ላይ ቢሊዮኖችን ያስቀምጣል። አንድ ከፍተኛ ደረጃ ያለው ቺፕ ከ500 እስከ 1,000 የሂደት ደረጃዎችንና ከ80 እስከ 120 ንብርብሮችን በሦስት እስከ አራት ወራት ውስጥ ያልፋል፤ ይህም አንዲት የአቧራ ቅንጣት ልታበላሸው በምትችልባቸው እጅግ ንጹሕ ክፍሎች ውስጥ ነው። ንድፍ የማተሙ ደረጃ የኔዘርላንድሱ ASML ብቻ የሚሰራውን «EUV» ማሽን ይፈልጋል፤ አንዱ 200 ሚሊዮን ዶላር ገደማ፣ አዲሱ «High-NA» ደግሞ 380 ሚሊዮን ዶላር ገደማ ያወጣል።

ሳይንሱ

የንጹሕ ሲሊከን ኬሚስትሪ፦

SiO₂ + 2C → Si + 2CO (የኤሌክትሪክ ምድጃ፣ ~2,000 °C፣ ~98–99% ንጹሕ)

Si + 3HCl → SiHCl₃ + H₂ (ሊጣራ የሚችል ፈሳሽ መሥራት)

SiHCl₃ + H₂ → Si + 3HCl (በሞቃት ዘንጎች ላይ፣ ~1,100 °C፦ 99.9999999%+)

ከዚያም ከቀለጠ ሲሊከን አንድ ክሪስታል በዝግታ ይጎተታል (የቾክራልስኪ ዘዴ)፤ ወደ ዌፈሮችም ይቆረጣል።

  1. Quartz → silicon

    Quartz and carbon are heated in an electric furnace to about 2,000 °C, giving silicon that is about 98–99% pure.

    ኳርትዝ → ሲሊከን

    ኳርትዝና ካርቦን በኤሌክትሪክ ምድጃ ውስጥ በ2,000 °C ገደማ ይሞቃሉ፤ 98–99% ንጹሕ ሲሊከን ይገኛል።

  2. Purify

    Chemistry raises purity to 99.9999999% or better ("nine nines"): fewer than one foreign atom in a billion.

    ማጣራት

    በኬሚካል ሂደት ንጽሕናው 99.9999999% ወይም ከዚያ በላይ ይደርሳል፤ ከአንድ ቢሊዮን አተሞች አንድ እንኳ ባዕድ አይኖርም።

  3. Grow a crystal

    A seed is slowly pulled from molten silicon to grow one perfect crystal, an "ingot" up to 2 metres long.

    ክሪስታል ማሳደግ

    ከቀለጠ ሲሊከን ውስጥ ዘር በዝግታ እየተጎተተ እስከ 2 ሜትር የሚረዝም አንድ ፍጹም ክሪስታል ይበቅላል።

  4. Slice into wafers

    The ingot is cut into discs less than 1 mm thick and polished like a mirror.

    ወደ ዌፈር መቁረጥ

    ክሪስታሉ ከ1 ሚሊሜትር ባነሰ ውፍረት ተቆርጦ እንደ መስታወት ይለሰልሳል።

  5. The layer loop (repeat!)

    Deposit a thin film → coat with light-sensitive resist → print the pattern with light → etch away what is not needed → add dopants. Repeat for every layer.

    የንብርብር ዑደት (ይደገማል!)

    ቀጭን ሽፋን ማስቀመጥ → ለብርሃን ስሱ በሆነ ቅባት መሸፈን → ንድፉን በብርሃን ማተም → የማያስፈልገውን መፋቅ → ዶፓንት መጨመር። ለእያንዳንዱ ንብርብር ይደገማል።

  6. Add the wiring

    Layers of copper wires, thinner than a virus, connect billions of transistors.

    ሽቦዎችን መዘርጋት

    ከቫይረስ የቀጠኑ የመዳብ ሽቦ ንብርብሮች ቢሊዮኖችን ትራንዚስተሮች ያገናኛሉ።

  7. Test and cut

    Each chip on the wafer is tested by machine, then the wafer is cut into individual chips ("dies").

    መፈተሽና መቁረጥ

    በዌፈሩ ላይ ያለ እያንዳንዱ ቺፕ በማሽን ይፈተሻል፤ ከዚያም ዌፈሩ ወደ ተናጠል ቺፖች ይቆረጣል።

  8. Package and test again

    Each die is sealed in a protective package with metal legs or balls, tested once more, then shipped.

    ማሸግና እንደገና መፈተሽ

    እያንዳንዱ ቺፕ የብረት እግሮች ባሉት መከላከያ ውስጥ ታሽጎ፣ አንድ ጊዜ ተጨማሪ ተፈትሾ ይላካል።

Founder's tool

Build in loops. A chip is built layer by layer, and every layer is checked before the next. Your project grows the same way: build, test, learn, improve, repeat. Each loop should be small and fast.

የሥራ ፈጣሪ መሣሪያ

በዑደት ይገንቡ። ቺፕ በንብርብር ይገነባል፤ እያንዳንዱ ንብርብር ከቀጣዩ በፊት ይፈተሻል። የእርስዎ ፕሮጀክትም በዚሁ መንገድ ያድጋል፦ ይገንቡ፣ ይፈትሹ፣ ይማሩ፣ ያሻሽሉ፣ ይድገሙ። እያንዳንዱ ዑደት ትንሽና ፈጣን ይሁን።

MODULE 6Add value at home · እሴትን በአገር ውስጥ ይጨምሩ

Who earns the money, and how to climb

True story

During a hospital internship in Yaoundé in 2010, engineer Arthur Zang saw how hard it was for Cameroonians to get heart tests: the country had only a few dozen cardiologists for more than 20 million people. He built the Cardiopad, a touch-screen medical tablet that records an electrocardiogram in a village clinic and sends it by mobile network to a distant cardiologist. It won the 2014 Rolex Award for Enterprise and the 2016 Africa Prize. At first its electronic parts were made in China and assembled in Cameroon; Zang said he hoped to manufacture it in Cameroon.

Arthur designed in Africa and assembled in Africa, then aimed to climb to manufacturing. That is the staircase every African product can climb. The further a material travels up the chain, the more it is worth: raw ore is cheapest, refined metal is worth far more, a finished chip far more again.

DR Congo mines 73% of the world's cobalt, but China produces 79% of the world's refined cobalt. Much of the value is captured at refining. Ethiopia now allows lithium and tantalum exports only by companies that add value inside the country.

ገንዘቡን የሚያገኘው ማነው? እንዴትስ ወደ ላይ መውጣት ይቻላል?

እውነተኛ ታሪክ

በ2010 በያውንዴ ሆስፒታል ተለማማጅ ሳለ መሐንዲስ አርተር ዛንግ ካሜሩናውያን የልብ ምርመራ ለማግኘት ምን ያህል እንደሚቸገሩ አየ፤ አገሪቱ ከ20 ሚሊዮን በላይ ሕዝብ ላይ ጥቂት ደርዘን የልብ ሐኪሞች ብቻ ነበሯት። «ካርዲዮፓድ» የተባለ በመንካት የሚሰራ የሕክምና ታብሌት ሠራ፤ በገጠር ክሊኒክ የልብ ምት ምርመራ (ECG) መዝግቦ በሞባይል ኔትወርክ ርቆ ወዳለ የልብ ሐኪም ይልካል። የ2014 የሮሌክስ የሥራ ፈጠራ ሽልማትንና የ2016 የአፍሪካ ሽልማትን አሸነፈ። መጀመሪያ ላይ የኤሌክትሮኒክ ክፍሎቹ በቻይና ተመርተው በካሜሩን ይገጣጠሙ ነበር፤ ዛንግ በካሜሩን ለማምረት ተስፋ እንዳለው ተናግሯል።

አርተር በአፍሪካ ነደፈ፣ በአፍሪካ ገጣጠመ፣ ከዚያም ወደ ማምረት ለመውጣት አለመ። እያንዳንዱ የአፍሪካ ምርት ሊወጣው የሚችለው ደረጃ ይህ ነው። አንድ ማዕድን በሰንሰለቱ ወደ ላይ በወጣ ቁጥር ዋጋው ይጨምራል፦ ጥሬ ማዕድን በጣም ርካሽ ነው፤ የተጣራ ብረት ብዙ እጥፍ ዋጋ አለው፤ የተጠናቀቀ ቺፕ ደግሞ ከዚያም በላይ።

Find where value is added, then climb. Ask: what happens to this product after it leaves my hands? Who processes, designs, assembles, packs or brands it? Every step up is a business.

MODULE 7Africans already building · እየገነቡ ያሉ አፍሪካውያን

The industry is already starting here

True story

Anthony Githinji worked in the US semiconductor industry from 1997, then returned to Kenya and founded Semiconductor Technologies Limited (STL) at Dedan Kimathi University of Technology in Nyeri. When COVID-19 stopped foreign experts from coming, the company built its factory with local talent, and it trains students as interns.

True story

Ethiopians are on the same path. In 2021, Pazion Cherinet (Orbit Health, a digital health platform) became the first Ethiopian shortlisted for the Africa Prize for Engineering Innovation. In 2023, mechanical engineer Fikru Gebre Dinkumbab was shortlisted for a multi-purpose earth-brick machine. In 2024, the Prize went to Kenya's Esther Kimani for an early-warning system that detects crop pests and diseases, with £50,000.

Africa still holds less than 1% of the global semiconductor industry, but it is not starting from zero. In April 2026 the first Africa Semiconductor Investors Forum in Nairobi named the realistic entry points: chip design, assembly, testing and packaging, mineral processing, skills and regional hubs.

ኢንዱስትሪው እዚህ መጀመሩ አይቀርም፤ ጀምሯልም

እውነተኛ ታሪክ

Check yourself

1. What did William Kamkwamba use to learn how to build his windmill?

A textbook called Using Energy from a small village library.

2. Why does a red LED need about three lemons?

Each lemon gives about 0.9 V; a red LED needs about 1.8–2 V.

3. What does boron do when added to silicon?

It leaves a missing electron (a hole), making p-type silicon.

4. In the dry-soil alarm, why does wet soil turn the LED off?

Wet soil conducts, pulling the transistor's base below about 0.6 V, so it switches off.

5. Why should you never burn cables to get copper?

The smoke is toxic.

6. Name one mineral found at Ethiopia's Kenticha deposit.

Tantalum (lithium is there too).
