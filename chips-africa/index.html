PART 1The science of a chip · የቺፕ ሳይንስ

How a semiconductor works

True story

Kelvin Doe grew up in Freetown, Sierra Leone, where the power came on only rarely. From about age 10 he searched scrap heaps for parts. When he realised all his money went on batteries, he built his own from acid, baking soda and metal. He built a sound mixer from scrap transistors, then a generator and an FM transmitter, and ran a community radio station as “DJ Focus”. In 2012, at 15, he became the youngest person invited to MIT's Visiting Practitioner programme.

Kelvin learned by taking things apart and testing them. To go further, from using parts to understanding them, you need the science inside every chip:

The science
  • Silicon has 4 outer electrons. In a pure crystal, each one is locked in a bond with a neighbour, so pure silicon conducts electricity poorly.
  • Doping: replace one atom in about a million with phosphorus (5 outer electrons) and a spare electron is free to move: n-type. Use boron (3) instead and you leave a gap, a hole, that also moves: p-type.
  • p-n junction: join p and n and current flows easily in one direction only. That is a diode. An LED is a diode that gives out light when electrons fall into holes.
  • Transistor: a small voltage on its control pin opens or closes a path for a bigger current. It is a switch with no moving parts. A modern chip holds billions of them.

Build 1: a fruit battery that proves the diode

You need: 3 lemons (or potatoes), 3 galvanised (zinc-coated) nails, 3 pieces of copper wire or copper coins, 4 short wires, and 1 red LED from an old toy or charger.

  1. Push one nail and one copper piece into each lemon, about 2 cm apart, not touching.
  2. Connect them in a chain: copper of lemon 1 to zinc of lemon 2, copper of lemon 2 to zinc of lemon 3.
  3. Touch the LED's long leg to the free copper end and its short leg to the free zinc end. In a dark room it glows faintly.
  4. Now turn the LED around. It stays dark. You have just seen a p-n junction allow current one way only.
The science

Zinc gives up electrons more easily than copper. The lemon juice (an acid) lets ions move between them, so electrons flow through the wire from zinc to copper. Each lemon gives about 0.9 volts. A red LED needs about 1.8–2 volts, so three lemons in series are just enough.

3 × 0.9 V ≈ 2.7 V > 1.8 V (red LED)

Stage 1: From sand to a perfect wafer

Every chip starts as quartz, silicon dioxide (SiO₂), the most common mineral in sand and rock. Turning it into a wafer takes five steps:

  1. Mine and select quartz. High-purity quartz rock is chosen and washed.
  2. Reduce it in a furnace. Quartz and carbon (coal, charcoal, wood chips) are heated in an electric arc furnace to about 2,000 °C. The carbon pulls the oxygen away, leaving silicon that is about 98–99% pure.
  3. Purify it. The silicon reacts with hydrogen chloride to make a liquid, trichlorosilane, that can be distilled like alcohol. The clean liquid then deposits silicon onto hot rods at about 1,100 °C. The result is 99.9999999% pure or better: fewer than one foreign atom in a billion.
  4. Grow one perfect crystal. The pure silicon is melted (it melts at 1,414 °C) in a quartz crucible. A small seed crystal is dipped in, turned and pulled up very slowly. The atoms line up behind it and form one single crystal, an ingot up to 300 mm wide and about 2 m long. This is the Czochralski method. A tiny amount of boron or phosphorus is added to the melt.
  5. Slice and polish. Diamond-coated wire cuts the ingot into wafers less than 1 mm thick (a 300 mm wafer is about 0.78 mm). They are ground, polished to a mirror and cleaned.

  4. አንድ ፍጹም ክሪስታል ማሳደግ። ንጹሑ ሲሊከን በኳርትዝ ማሰሮ ውስጥ ይቀልጣል (የሚቀልጠው በ1,414 °C ነው)። ትንሽ የዘር ክሪስታል ተነክሮ እየተሽከረከረ በጣም በዝግታ ይጎተታል። አተሞቹ ከኋላው ተሰልፈው እስከ 300 ሚ.ሜ ስፋትና 2 ሜትር ገደማ ርዝመት ያለው አንድ ክሪስታል ይሠራሉ። ይህ የቾክራልስኪ ዘዴ ነው። በቀለጠው ሲሊከን ላይ በጣም ትንሽ ቦሮን ወይም ፎስፈረስ ይጨመራል።
  5. መቁረጥና ማለስለስ። በአልማዝ የተለበጠ ሽቦ ክሪስታሉን ከ1 ሚ.ሜ ባነሰ ውፍረት ወደ ዌፈሮች ይቆርጣል (የ300 ሚ.ሜ ዌፈር 0.78 ሚ.ሜ ገደማ ነው)። ዌፈሮቹ ተፈጭተው እንደ መስታወት ይለሰልሳሉ፤ ይጸዳሉም።

The whole factory journey at a glance · የፋብሪካው ጉዞ በአጭሩ

  1. Quartz → silicon

    Quartz and carbon are heated in an electric furnace to about 2,000 °C, giving silicon that is about 98–99% pure.

    ኳርትዝ → ሲሊከን

    ኳርትዝና ካርቦን በኤሌክትሪክ ምድጃ ውስጥ በ2,000 °C ገደማ ይሞቃሉ፤ 98–99% ንጹሕ ሲሊከን ይገኛል።

  2. Purify

    Chemistry raises purity to 99.9999999% or better ("nine nines"): fewer than one foreign atom in a billion.

    ማጣራት

    በኬሚካል ሂደት ንጽሕናው 99.9999999% ወይም ከዚያ በላይ ይደርሳል፤ ከአንድ ቢሊዮን አተሞች አንድ እንኳ ባዕድ አይኖርም።

  3. Grow a crystal

    A seed is slowly pulled from molten silicon to grow one perfect crystal, an "ingot" up to 2 metres long.

    ክሪስታል ማሳደግ

    ከቀለጠ ሲሊከን ውስጥ ዘር በዝግታ እየተጎተተ እስከ 2 ሜትር የሚረዝም አንድ ፍጹም ክሪስታል ይበቅላል።

  4. Slice into wafers

    The ingot is cut into discs less than 1 mm thick and polished like a mirror.

    ወደ ዌፈር መቁረጥ

    ክሪስታሉ ከ1 ሚሊሜትር ባነሰ ውፍረት ተቆርጦ እንደ መስታወት ይለሰልሳል።

  5. The layer loop (repeat!)

    Deposit a thin film → coat with light-sensitive resist → print the pattern with light → etch away what is not needed → add dopants. Repeat for every layer.

    የንብርብር ዑደት (ይደገማል!)

    ቀጭን ሽፋን ማስቀመጥ → ለብርሃን ስሱ በሆነ ቅባት መሸፈን → ንድፉን በብርሃን ማተም → የማያስፈልገውን መፋቅ → ዶፓንት መጨመር። ለእያንዳንዱ ንብርብር ይደገማል።

  6. Add the wiring

    Layers of copper wires, thinner than a virus, connect billions of transistors.

    ሽቦዎችን መዘርጋት

    ከቫይረስ የቀጠኑ የመዳብ ሽቦ ንብርብሮች ቢሊዮኖችን ትራንዚስተሮች ያገናኛሉ።

  7. Test and cut

    Each chip on the wafer is tested by machine, then the wafer is cut into individual chips ("dies").

    መፈተሽና መቁረጥ

    በዌፈሩ ላይ ያለ እያንዳንዱ ቺፕ በማሽን ይፈተሻል፤ ከዚያም ዌፈሩ ወደ ተናጠል ቺፖች ይቆረጣል።

  8. Package and test again

    Each die is sealed in a protective package with metal legs or balls, tested once more, then shipped.

    ማሸግና እንደገና መፈተሽ

    እያንዳንዱ ቺፕ የብረት እግሮች ባሉት መከላከያ ውስጥ ታሽጎ፣ አንድ ጊዜ ተጨማሪ ተፈትሾ ይላካል።

STAGE 2Building the chip · ቺፑን መገንባት

Stage 2: Building the chip, layer by layer

The wafer now goes into a fab, a factory whose cleanroom air is filtered so that almost no dust remains. Wafers travel between machines in sealed boxes. On each wafer, hundreds of identical chips are built at the same time, one thin layer after another. Every layer goes through the same loop:

  1. Clean. Ultra-pure water and acids remove every particle.
  2. Grow or deposit a film. Heating in oxygen grows a thin insulating layer of SiO₂; other machines deposit metals or insulators, from a few atoms to a few micrometres thick.
  3. Coat. The wafer spins while a light-sensitive liquid, photoresist, spreads into an even film.
  4. Expose (lithography). Light shines through a mask carrying the circuit pattern; lenses or mirrors shrink the image onto the wafer. Older layers use deep-ultraviolet light (193 nm); the finest use extreme-ultraviolet light (13.5 nm) made by hitting tiny tin droplets with a laser. Only ASML in the Netherlands builds EUV machines: about $200 million each, and about $380 million for the newest High-NA version.
  5. Develop. A chemical washes away the exposed resist, leaving the pattern.
  6. Etch. Plasma or chemicals cut away the film wherever the resist is not protecting it.
  7. Dope. Ion implantation fires boron, phosphorus or arsenic ions into the silicon to make p-type and n-type regions; heating to about 1,000 °C activates them.
  8. Strip, clean and inspect. The old resist is removed and machines check the layer before the next pass.

The first passes build the transistors (the front end). The later passes build more than ten levels of copper wiring (the back end): trenches are etched, filled with copper and polished flat by chemical-mechanical polishing (CMP). In total a leading-edge chip passes through 500 to 1,000 process steps and 80 to 120 layers, over three to four months.

ዌፈሩ አሁን ወደ «ፋብ» ይገባል፤ ይህ የንጹሕ ክፍሉ አየር አቧራ እስከማይቀር ድረስ የሚጣራበት ፋብሪካ ነው። ዌፈሮች በታሸጉ ሳጥኖች ውስጥ በማሽኖች መካከል ይጓዛሉ። በእያንዳንዱ ዌፈር ላይ በመቶዎች የሚቆጠሩ ተመሳሳይ ቺፖች በአንድ ጊዜ፣ አንዱ ቀጭን ንብርብር ከሌላው በኋላ ይገነባሉ። እያንዳንዱ ንብርብር ያንኑ ዑደት ያልፋል፦

  1. ማጽዳት። እጅግ ንጹሕ ውሃና አሲዶች እያንዳንዷን ቅንጣት ያስወግዳሉ።
  2. ሽፋን ማሳደግ ወይም ማስቀመጥ። በኦክስጅን ውስጥ ማሞቅ ቀጭን የSiO₂ መከላከያ ንብርብር ያሳድጋል፤ ሌሎች ማሽኖች ደግሞ ከጥቂት አተሞች እስከ ጥቂት ማይክሮሜትሮች ውፍረት ያላቸው ብረቶችን ወይም መከላከያዎችን ያስቀምጣሉ።
  3. መቀባት። ዌፈሩ ሲሽከረከር ለብርሃን ስሱ የሆነ «ፎቶሬዚስት» ፈሳሽ እኩል ሽፋን ሆኖ ይዘረጋል።
  4. ለብርሃን ማጋለጥ (ሊቶግራፊ)። ብርሃን የወረዳ ንድፍ በያዘ ጭምብል ውስጥ ያልፋል፤ ሌንሶች ወይም መስታወቶች ምስሉን አሳንሰው በዌፈሩ ላይ ያሳርፋሉ። የቆዩ ንብርብሮች ጥልቅ-አልትራቫዮሌት ብርሃን (193 nm) ይጠቀማሉ፤ በጣም ጥቃቅኖቹ ደግሞ ጥቃቅን የቆርቆሮ ጠብታዎችን በሌዘር በመምታት የሚፈጠር እጅግ-አልትራቫዮሌት (EUV፣ 13.5 nm) ብርሃን ይጠቀማሉ። የEUV ማሽኖችን የሚሠራው የኔዘርላንድሱ ASML ብቻ ነው፤ አንዱ 200 ሚሊዮን ዶላር ገደማ፣ አዲሱ «High-NA» ደግሞ 380 ሚሊዮን ዶላር ገደማ ያወጣል።
  5. ማጠብ (develop)። ኬሚካል ለብርሃን የተጋለጠውን ሬዚስት አጥቦ ንድፉን ያስቀራል።
  6. መፋቅ (etch)። ፕላዝማ ወይም ኬሚካሎች ሬዚስቱ ያልጠበቀውን ሽፋን ቆርጠው ያስወግዳሉ።
  7. ዶፒንግ። «አዮን ኢምፕላንቴሽን» የቦሮን፣ የፎስፈረስ ወይም የአርሰኒክ ion-ዎችን ወደ ሲሊከኑ ይተኩሳል፤ p-ዓይነትና n-ዓይነት ቦታዎችን ይፈጥራል፤ በ1,000 °C ገደማ ማሞቅ ያነቃቸዋል።
  8. ማስወገድ፣ ማጽዳትና መመርመር። የቀረው ሬዚስት ይወገዳል፤ ማሽኖች ንብርብሩን ከቀጣዩ ዙር በፊት ይፈትሻሉ።

የመጀመሪያዎቹ ዙሮች ትራንዚስተሮቹን ይገነባሉ (የፊት ክፍል)። ቀጣዮቹ ዙሮች ከአሥር በላይ የመዳብ ሽቦ ደረጃዎችን ይገነባሉ (የኋላ ክፍል)፦ ቦዮች ተቆፍረው በመዳብ ይሞላሉ፤ ከዚያም በኬሚካላዊ-ሜካኒካዊ ማለስለሻ (CMP) ጠፍጣፋ ይሆናሉ። በአጠቃላይ ከፍተኛ ደረጃ ያለው ቺፕ ከ500 እስከ 1,000 የሂደት ደረጃዎችንና ከ80 እስከ 120 ንብርብሮችን በሦስት እስከ አራት ወራት ውስጥ ያልፋል።

STAGE 3Test, cut, package · መፈተሽ፣ መቁረጥ፣ ማሸግ

Stage 3: Test, cut and package

  1. Wafer test. Fine needles touch every chip on the wafer and run electrical tests. The share of chips that work is called the yield.
  2. Dicing. A diamond saw or laser cuts the wafer into individual chips, called dies.
  3. Attach and connect. Each good die is fixed onto a small base (a substrate) and connected to it with thin gold or copper wires, or with tiny solder bumps when the die is flipped upside down (flip-chip).
  4. Seal. The die is covered in hard plastic (epoxy) or a metal lid. Metal legs or a grid of solder balls underneath will later connect it to a circuit board.
  5. Final test. Packaged chips are heated, stressed and sorted by speed. Some packages now stack several dies together; this advanced packaging is a bottleneck for AI chips worldwide.

Africa already does this stage: STMicroelectronics has run a chip assembly and test plant at Bouskoura, near Casablanca in Morocco, for decades.

  3. ማጣበቅና ማገናኘት። እያንዳንዱ ጥሩ ዳይ በትንሽ መሠረት ላይ ይጣበቃል፤ በቀጭን የወርቅ ወይም የመዳብ ሽቦዎች፣ ወይም ዳዩ ተገልብጦ በጥቃቅን የሶልደር ነጥቦች ይገናኛል።

አፍሪካ ይህንን ደረጃ ቀድሞውኑ ትሠራለች፦ STMicroelectronics በሞሮኮ ካዛብላንካ አቅራቢያ ቡስኩራ ውስጥ ለአሥርት ዓመታት የቺፕ መገጣጠሚያና መፈተሻ ፋብሪካ ሲያንቀሳቅስ ቆይቷል።

STAGE 4Chip → circuit board · ቺፕ → የወረዳ ሰሌዳ

Stage 4: From chips to a circuit board

A chip alone does nothing. It must be soldered onto a printed circuit board (PCB) together with other parts.

Making the board

  1. Layers. Sheets of fibreglass and epoxy (called FR-4) are covered with thin copper foil.
  2. Pattern the copper. The same idea as in the fab, at a bigger scale: the copper is coated, exposed to light through a pattern, and etched, leaving copper tracks.
  3. Stack, drill and plate. Layers are pressed together, holes are drilled and plated with copper to connect the layers.
  4. Finish. A green solder mask protects the tracks and white printing labels each part.

Assembling the board

  1. Print solder paste. A metal stencil spreads solder paste onto each pad.
  2. Place the parts. A pick-and-place machine sets chips, resistors and capacitors onto the paste, thousands per hour.
  3. Reflow. An oven melts the solder (lead-free solder melts at about 217–220 °C), fixing every part at once.
  4. Inspect and test. Cameras check each joint, larger parts such as connectors are added, and the board is tested and loaded with its first program (firmware).

Device assembly is growing in Africa: local plants in Egypt, Nigeria, Ethiopia and Rwanda now assemble devices, and Arthur Zang's Cardiopad was assembled in Cameroon.

You can do this stage yourself at small scale: design a board with the free program KiCad, and solder parts by hand with a soldering iron.

  1. ንብርብሮች። የፋይበርግላስና የኢፖክሲ ሉሆች (FR-4 ይባላሉ) በቀጭን የመዳብ ሽፋን ይለበጣሉ።
  2. የመዳቡን ንድፍ ማውጣት። በፋቡ ውስጥ ያለው ሐሳብ በትልቅ መጠን፦ መዳቡ ተቀብቶ በንድፍ በኩል ለብርሃን ይጋለጣል፣ ይፋቃልም፤ የመዳብ መስመሮች ይቀራሉ።
  3. መደራረብ፣ መብሳትና መለበጥ። ንብርብሮቹ ተጭነው ይጣበቃሉ፤ ቀዳዳዎች ተበስተው ንብርብሮቹን ለማገናኘት በመዳብ ይለበጣሉ።

  2. ዕቃዎቹን ማስቀመጥ። «ፒክ-ኤንድ-ፕሌስ» ማሽን ቺፖችን፣ ሬዚስተሮችንና ካፓሲተሮችን በሊጡ ላይ በሰዓት በሺዎች ያስቀምጣል።

STAGE 5Board → computer · ሰሌዳ → ኮምፒውተር

Stage 5: From boards to a complete computer

A computer is a team of chips and parts, each with one job:

  1. Processor (CPU). Runs the instructions of every program. Its clock ticks billions of times a second (GHz).
  2. Memory (RAM). Holds the data the CPU is working on. Each bit is a tiny charge on a capacitor beside one transistor; it is lost when power is switched off.
  3. Storage (SSD, flash, hard disk). Keeps files when power is off. Flash memory traps electric charge in its transistors; hard disks store data magnetically.
  4. Graphics processor (GPU). Thousands of small cores draw the screen and, today, run AI.
  5. Motherboard. The large circuit board whose copper tracks (buses) connect all the parts.
  6. Power supply. Turns 220 V alternating current from the wall into 12 V, 5 V and 3.3 V direct current; chips on the board lower it further.
  7. Input and output. Keyboard, touch screen, camera and sensors bring information in; display, speaker and network send it out.
  8. Firmware and software. When you press power, firmware (BIOS/UEFI) wakes up, checks the hardware and loads the operating system, which then runs your apps.

In a phone, the CPU, GPU and other blocks are combined on one system-on-chip. Apple's A16 Bionic, for example, holds 16 billion transistors.

ደረጃ 5፦ ከሰሌዳዎች እስከ ሙሉ ኮምፒውተር

ኮምፒውተር እያንዳንዳቸው አንድ ሥራ ያላቸው የቺፖችና የዕቃዎች ቡድን ነው፦

  1. ፕሮሰሰር (ሲፒዩ)። የእያንዳንዱን ፕሮግራም መመሪያዎች ይፈጽማል። ሰዓቱ በሰከንድ በቢሊዮን ጊዜ ይመታል (GHz)።
  2. ማህደረ ትውስታ (RAM)። ሲፒዩው እየሠራበት ያለውን መረጃ ይይዛል። እያንዳንዱ ቢት ከአንድ ትራንዚስተር አጠገብ ባለ ትንሽ ካፓሲተር ላይ ያለ ትንሽ ቻርጅ ነው፤ ኃይል ሲጠፋ ይጠፋል።
  3. ማከማቻ (SSD፣ ፍላሽ፣ ሃርድ ዲስክ)። ኃይል ሲጠፋም ፋይሎችን ያስቀምጣል። ፍላሽ ማህደረ ትውስታ ኤሌክትሪክ ቻርጅን በትራንዚስተሮቹ ውስጥ ያጠምዳል፤ ሃርድ ዲስኮች መረጃን በማግኔት ያስቀምጣሉ።
  4. የግራፊክስ ፕሮሰሰር (ጂፒዩ)። በሺዎች የሚቆጠሩ ትናንሽ ኮሮች ስክሪኑን ይስላሉ፤ ዛሬ ደግሞ AI ያንቀሳቅሳሉ።
  5. ማዘርቦርድ። የመዳብ መስመሮቹ (ባሶች) ሁሉንም ክፍሎች የሚያገናኙበት ትልቁ የወረዳ ሰሌዳ።
  6. የኃይል አቅራቢ። ከግድግዳ የሚመጣውን 220 V ተለዋጭ ኤሌክትሪክ ወደ 12 V፣ 5 V እና 3.3 V ቀጥተኛ ኤሌክትሪክ ይቀይራል፤ በሰሌዳው ላይ ያሉ ቺፖች ደግሞ የበለጠ ይቀንሱታል።
  7. ግብዓትና ውጤት። የቁልፍ ሰሌዳ፣ የንክኪ ስክሪን፣ ካሜራና ዳሳሾች መረጃ ያስገባሉ፤ ስክሪን፣ ስፒከርና ኔትወርክ ያስወጣሉ።
  8. ፈርምዌርና ሶፍትዌር። የማብሪያ ቁልፉን ሲጫኑ ፈርምዌሩ (BIOS/UEFI) ይነቃል፣ ሃርድዌሩን ይፈትሻል፣ ኦፐሬቲንግ ሲስተሙን ይጭናል፤ እሱም መተግበሪያዎችዎን ያስኬዳል።

በስልክ ውስጥ ሲፒዩ፣ ጂፒዩና ሌሎች ክፍሎች በአንድ «ሲስተም-ኦን-ቺፕ» ላይ ይጣመራሉ። ለምሳሌ የአፕል A16 Bionic 16 ቢሊዮን ትራንዚስተሮችን ይዟል።

  1. Gates make an adder. An XOR gate and an AND gate together add two binary digits (1 + 1 = 10 in binary: sum 0, carry 1). Chain them and you can add any numbers.

Safety first

You may open an old desktop computer (unplugged) to identify the motherboard, CPU, RAM, storage and power supply. Never open the power supply box itself: its capacitors can hold a dangerous charge.

Founder's tool

Understand the whole system. Founders who understand every layer, from materials to software, spot opportunities others miss: a cheaper part, a local repair business, a sensor that fills a gap.

Start with a real problem

True story

In 2001–2002, a severe drought and famine hit Malawi. William Kamkwamba, 14, had to leave school because his family could not pay the fees. He kept learning in a small village library, where he found a textbook called Using Energy with a picture of a wind turbine. From a bicycle dynamo, a tractor fan, blue-gum poles and other scrap, he built a windmill that lit four bulbs in his home, with homemade switches and a circuit breaker made from nails. A later windmill pumped water to irrigate crops.

William did not start with money, a lab or a degree. He started with a problem his family felt every day (darkness and hunger), a library book and the scrap around him. That is the first rule of building: a real problem plus what you have.

The science

A bicycle dynamo is a small generator. When a magnet spins near a coil of copper wire, the changing magnetic field pushes electrons along the wire. This is electromagnetic induction, discovered by Michael Faraday in 1831. Wind turns the blades, the blades turn the magnet, and motion becomes electricity.

The materials, and the treasure in waste

True story

Near Lubumbashi in DR Congo stands Big Hill, about 10 million tonnes of slag left by a century of mining. In 2024, Gécamines' subsidiary STL began recovering germanium from it at a new local plant, with technical support from the Belgian company Umicore, and exported the first concentrates processed inside the country. What others had thrown away became a chip material.

Your own mine: old electronics

Every broken radio, phone charger, toy or computer is a box of free parts: transistors, resistors, LEDs, capacitors, motors, magnets, switches, wire and small speakers. Read the codes printed on transistors (for example BC547, 2N2222 or C1815) and look them up online to learn what they do.

Safety first
  • Never burn cables or circuit boards to get copper. The smoke is toxic.
  • Never open microwave ovens, old tube TVs or power supplies that were plugged in recently. They hold charges that can kill.
  • Do not puncture, bend or heat lithium batteries. Swollen batteries can catch fire.
  • Old solder contains lead: work with air flowing and wash your hands after.
  • Only work with batteries (up to 12 volts), never with the 220-volt mains.

Build a dry-soil alarm from scrap

True story

Brian Gitta, a computer science student at Makerere University in Uganda, kept missing lectures because of malaria, and once it took four blood tests to diagnose him. With his team he built Matibabu, a device that shines a red light through a finger and sends a result to a phone, with no blood needed. In 2018, at 24, he won the Royal Academy of Engineering's Africa Prize for Engineering Innovation (£25,000). His team was honest that the fifth prototype was about 80% accurate, and kept improving it.

Brian turned a personal pain into a sensor. You can start the same way with farming. The circuit below tells a farmer when the soil is dry. It uses one transistor and no microcontroller, and every part can come from scrap.

The science

Water in soil carries dissolved salts (ions), so wet soil conducts electricity and dry soil almost does not. R1 and the soil form a voltage divider at the transistor's base. A silicon transistor switches on when its base is above about 0.6 V.

Vbase = 4.5 V × Rsoil ÷ (R1 + Rsoil)

Wet soil, say 5 kΩ: 4.5 × 5 ÷ 52 ≈ 0.43 V → transistor off → LED off.
Dry soil, say 500 kΩ: the divider would give 4.5 × 500 ÷ 547 ≈ 4.1 V, far above 0.6 V, so current flows into the base, the transistor turns on (its base then settles near 0.7 V) and the LED lights.

The LED resistor follows Ohm's law (V = I × R): (4.5 V − 2 V) ÷ 220 Ω ≈ 11 mA, a safe current for a red LED.

Build 2: step by step

  1. Identify the transistor's pins (C, B, E) from its code and datasheet.
  2. Connect E to the battery's minus (−).
  3. Connect R1 (47 kΩ) from plus (+) to B.
  4. Connect one nail wire to B and the other nail wire to minus (−).
  5. Connect R2 (220 Ω) from plus (+) to the LED's long leg, and the LED's short leg to C.
  6. Push both nails 5 cm deep into soil, about 5 cm apart. Test in a cup of dry soil, then add water.

Tuning: if the LED stays on even in wet soil, use a larger R1 (100 kΩ). If it never lights in dry soil, use a smaller R1 (22 kΩ). Soils differ, so testing is part of the science.

The science

Why the nails rust: direct current through wet soil causes electrolysis, which corrodes the probes. Add the push button so power flows only when you check. Galvanised or stainless nails last longer.

Who earns the money, and how to climb

True story

During a hospital internship in Yaoundé in 2010, engineer Arthur Zang saw how hard it was for Cameroonians to get heart tests: the country had only a few dozen cardiologists for more than 20 million people. He built the Cardiopad, a touch-screen medical tablet that records an electrocardiogram in a village clinic and sends it by mobile network to a distant cardiologist. It won the 2014 Rolex Award for Enterprise and the 2016 Africa Prize. At first its electronic parts were made in China and assembled in Cameroon; Zang said he hoped to manufacture it in Cameroon.

Arthur designed in Africa and assembled in Africa, then aimed to climb to manufacturing. That is the staircase every African product can climb. The further a material travels up the chain, the more it is worth: raw ore is cheapest, refined metal is worth far more, a finished chip far more again.

DR Congo mines 73% of the world's cobalt, but China produces 79% of the world's refined cobalt. Much of the value is captured at refining. Ethiopia now allows lithium and tantalum exports only by companies that add value inside the country.

Check yourself

1. What did William Kamkwamba use to learn how to build his windmill?

A textbook called Using Energy from a small village library.

2. Why does a red LED need about three lemons?

Each lemon gives about 0.9 V; a red LED needs about 1.8–2 V.

3. What does boron do when added to silicon?

It leaves a missing electron (a hole), making p-type silicon.

4. In the dry-soil alarm, why does wet soil turn the LED off?

Wet soil conducts, pulling the transistor's base below about 0.6 V, so it switches off.

5. Why should you never burn cables to get copper?

The smoke is toxic.

6. Name one mineral found at Ethiopia's Kenticha deposit.

Tantalum (lithium is there too).

7. In the Czochralski method, how is a single crystal made?

A seed crystal is dipped into molten silicon, turned and pulled up slowly so atoms line up behind it.

8. Name the steps of the layer loop.

Clean, deposit a film, coat with resist, expose with light, develop, etch, dope, strip and inspect, then repeat.

9. What is the difference between RAM and storage?

RAM holds working data and forgets it when power is off; storage keeps files without power.
